# Local SEO & Google Business Profile Audit

Local ranking is a different algorithm: relevance, distance, and prominence,
weighted heavily toward the profile itself and the review corpus — not the
website. An SEO who treats GBP as an afterthought loses local clients. The
profile *is* the landing page for the map pack.

## Inputs

- Business name, primary category, and the exact address (or service-area list)
- The GBP URL or the business's Maps listing
- 3–5 local competitors that rank in the pack for the core terms
- The 3–5 "money" search terms + the city/area they want to rank in
- Website URL (for on-site local signals + schema)

If you only have the business name and city, search Maps for the core term and
identify the current pack occupants before auditing. You can't assess prominence
without knowing who's winning.

## The audit, in priority order

### 1. Categories (the highest-leverage lever)
The **primary category** is the single biggest GBP ranking factor most businesses
get wrong. Check it against what the top pack competitors use for the target term
— search the term, inspect the ranking profiles' categories. Recommend the primary
that matches intent, plus every relevant secondary category. A wrong primary
category caps everything else.

### 2. NAP consistency
Name, Address, Phone must be byte-identical across GBP, the website, and major
citations. Inconsistency (Suite vs Ste, (416) vs 416) fragments prominence
signals. List every inconsistency found and the canonical version to standardize on.

### 3. Reviews — velocity, recency, sentiment, response
Local prominence tracks review *velocity and recency* more than raw count. Assess:
reviews in the last 90 days vs competitors, average rating, sentiment themes
(mine the text for recurring complaints/praise — these are product and content
insights, not just stars), and owner-response rate. Recommend a realistic
review-generation cadence, not "get more reviews".

### 4. Profile completeness & freshness
Hours (incl. special hours), services/products populated, attributes, a Q&A
section that's seeded and monitored, photos added recently (recency signals an
active business), and GBP Posts cadence. Score each and rank the gaps.

### 5. Local pack visibility (grid-aware)
Rankings vary by the searcher's physical location. A profile can rank #1 at the
storefront and vanish two miles away. If the user has a local-grid tool
(e.g., Local Falcon), use it; otherwise recommend spot-checks from different
points and be explicit that a single-point rank check is misleading for local.

### 6. On-site local signals
Location/service pages with unique content per location (not doorway
duplicates), embedded map, NAP in the footer, `LocalBusiness` schema with correct
`geo`, `areaServed`, and matching NAP. For multi-location, a proper location
hierarchy.

### 7. Competitor prominence gap
For each pack competitor: category, review count/velocity, and rough citation
footprint. Identify the specific gap between the client and whoever holds the top
pack spot. "They have 340 reviews at 4.8 with 20/month; you have 60 at 4.6 with
2/month" is an actionable prominence gap.

```
Canonical NAP + every inconsistency to correct, by source.

## Review strategy
Current vs competitor velocity, sentiment themes, response gap, target cadence.

## On-site local
Schema and location-page fixes.

## Competitor prominence gap
Table + the specific closing move.

## 90-day local plan
Ranked actions with owners; separate "profile" from "website" from "reviews".
```

## Rules

- Category first. If the primary category is wrong, nothing else matters yet.
- Never assess local rank from one location and call it "the ranking". State the
  grid caveat every time.
- Mine review *text*, not just the star average — it's free voice-of-customer
  data that feeds content, CRO, and service fixes.
- For service-area businesses, don't recommend a fake storefront address. Ever.
  Service-area configuration is the correct, policy-compliant answer.
