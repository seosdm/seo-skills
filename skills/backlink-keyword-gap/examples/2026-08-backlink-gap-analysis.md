# Backlink Gap Analysis — worked example

**Subject:** anonymised — `clientdomain.com`, e-commerce/retail, global English-speaking
**Competitors:** three, anonymised as `competitor1–3.com`
**Scope:** the backlink gap sheet — domains linking to competitors but not to you
**Date:** July 2026

---

## What a backlink gap actually is

One sentence: **the list of domains that link to your competitors and not to you.**

That's it. It is the least glamorous output in SEO and usually the most immediately
actionable, because every row is a site that has already demonstrated it will link to
something in your category. You aren't cold-pitching a stranger — you're pitching someone
who has already said yes to your competitor.

The metric is the **gap score**: competitor links from that domain, minus your links from
it. Positive means they're ahead there. Zero means no gap. Negative means you're ahead and
it isn't an opportunity.

## How to read it without wasting a quarter

**Sort by gap score, not by DR.** The instinct is to chase the highest domain rating on the
page. Wikipedia is DR 99 and it's the worst target in the table — you don't pitch Wikipedia,
you become notable enough to be cited by it. Forbes and TechCrunch are DR 93+ and both are
PR problems requiring a genuine news hook, not link problems.

The two best rows are **moz.com and hubspot.com at gap score 11** — and note that you
already have links from both. A domain that has linked to you once is dramatically easier to
get a second link from than one that never has. **Existing relationships beat cold outreach,
and the gap table is where you find them.**

**Read the negative row as a win and move on.** `github.com` shows −3: you have five links,
competitors have fewer. That's not an opportunity, it's a strength. It appears in the table
only because the analysis is symmetrical, and half the value of this report is knowing which
rows to ignore.

**Group by link type before you plan outreach.** The status column splits into Editorial,
Resource, Tool/Directory, PR, Community and Blog Platform — and those are completely
different acquisition motions. A resource-page link is an email. An editorial link is a
pitch. A PR link needs a story. Planning them as one campaign called "link building" is how
outreach quarters die.

## The finding that isn't in the gap table

Running alongside the gap analysis, the anchor text distribution shows:

| Anchor type | Yours | Industry ideal | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Branded | 39% | 30–40% | Healthy |
| **Exact match** | **20%** | **5–15%** | **Over-optimised** |
| Partial match | 14% | 10–20% | Healthy |
| Generic | 14% | 15–25% | Healthy |
| Naked URL | 4% | 3–8% | Healthy |

**Exact-match anchors at 20% against a 5–15% benchmark changes the order of the work.**

Building 15 new links from the gap table with keyword-rich anchors would take an existing
over-optimisation problem and make it materially worse. The correct sequence is: fix the
anchor mix first, then acquire — and specify branded or partial-match anchors on every new
placement until the ratio comes back inside the band.

This is why a gap table alone is a dangerous artefact. It tells you what to acquire. It says
nothing about whether acquiring more of the same thing is safe.

## Output: a 90-day plan

Derived from the table rather than from a template.

**Days 1–30.** Stop building exact-match anchors immediately. Approach moz.com and
hubspot.com — existing relationships, highest gap scores. Submit to semrush.com's tool
directory, which is a form rather than a pitch and the single easiest row in the table.

**Days 31–60.** Pitch backlinko.com and contentmarketinginstitute.com — both have resource
pages or contributor programmes, both are gap score 5+. Begin a broken-link campaign against
DR 60+ sites in the niche.

**Days 61–90.** Original data study, then use it as the hook for the PR rows — forbes.com
and techcrunch.com are unreachable without one and straightforward with one.

**Target:** 15+ new referring domains in 90 days, and exact-match anchors below 15%. The
second number matters more than the first, and it's the one that usually goes unmeasured.

## Method note

**The data in this example is a template with representative values**, not a live client
account — client and competitor domains are placeholders in the source. The referring
domains are real sites; the link counts against them are illustrative.

That makes it a valid demonstration of the *method and the output format*, and not a claim
about a specific client result. Worth stating plainly, because a gap analysis is exactly the
kind of deliverable where invented numbers are impossible to detect from the outside.

Run against real Ahrefs or Semrush exports, the table shape is identical and the reading
rules above are unchanged.
